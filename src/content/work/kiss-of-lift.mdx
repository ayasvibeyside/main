## Context

Kiss of Lift is a workout tracker I’m designing and building right now. It isn’t out yet, so this page is about what it’s for and the choices I’m making, not results.

## The problem

Most tracking apps work while everything goes to plan, and fail the moment it doesn’t. A bad night’s sleep, a busy week or a missed session, and the plan stops fitting the person using it. Many also assume you own a particular wearable, or ask you to wear one at all.

## Decisions

### Make logging seamless

Logging a set should take seconds and never get in the way of the lift. Every extra tap between sets is a reason to stop tracking.

### Work with your wearable, or none

Kiss of Lift connects to the wearable you prefer. If you don’t use one, nothing is lost: the app is complete on its own, and a wearable only adds to it.

### Build progressive overload in

Progress is the point of tracking, so the app helps you add load over time instead of leaving you to work it out from a spreadsheet.

### Plan for the hard weeks

Deloading is part of training, not a failure. The app lets you deload on purpose, and gives you a quick alternative version of a workout when time or energy is short, so a rough day becomes a shorter session instead of a skipped one.

## The product angle

The bet is that consistency beats perfection. People rarely quit because a plan was too easy. They quit when one missed week makes the whole plan feel broken. Designing the deload and the quick version as first-class paths, not workarounds, is how Kiss of Lift tries to keep people training.

## Outcome

Not launched yet. I’ll add what I learn here after the first people use it.

## What I’d do next

Test the quick version and the deload flow with real lifters, and decide which of them people reach for first.
