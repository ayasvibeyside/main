## Context

Thndr built its name making investing simple for more than 4 million first-time investors. ThndrX is the opposite brief: a platform for serious, high-frequency traders who want advanced tools, precision and speed. As Thndr’s CEO put it at launch, “it’s about respecting the craft.”

ThndrX was unveiled at Thndr’s 2025 product keynote, alongside the company’s $15.7M Series A.

## The problem

Designing for experts means showing more information, not less, without turning the screen into noise. That makes it a prioritisation problem. Every element has to earn its place on a dense screen: what’s visible, what’s one tap away, and which risk controls have to sit inline at the exact moment a trader decides.

## Decisions

### Put risk controls inside the order ticket

The order ticket handles market and limit orders, with Take Profit and Stop Loss inline rather than as a separate step. It also covers conditions like All-or-None and order expiry, and shows a transparent fee breakdown: Thndr’s fee, third-party fees and the total.

### Give every position its full story

Position management is split into Full Trades, Partial Sells, Positions and Analytics. Each holding shows its entry-to-exit window, how long it was held, the number of orders, and profit or loss in both percent and EGP.

### Build a market cockpit for the whole session

One view brings together the EGX 30, 70, 100 and Shariah indices, 24K gold, USD/EGP, advancers and decliners, and sector performance. Data widgets compare today’s volume with yesterday and with the average.

### Lock the palette so the product can scale

A full light and dark system, with colours split into locked and suggested sets, keeps the product consistent as more teams build on it.

## The product angle

The hardest calls weren’t visual. They were about triage between what experts need, how fast they need it, and how much the screen can hold before it slows them down. That balance between user need, speed and restraint is the skill I’m bringing into product management.
